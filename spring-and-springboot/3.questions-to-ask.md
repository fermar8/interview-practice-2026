# Questions to Ask During the Get-to-Know Interview

## About the Product & Business Context

### Understanding What You're Building
- **What's the main problem your team is solving for Otto Group right now?** (Shows you care about business impact)
- **What does success look like for your team in the next 6-12 months?**
- **Are there any features or initiatives you're particularly excited about launching soon?**
- **How does your team's work impact other teams at Otto Group, or end customers directly?**
- **What's changed about your product strategy in the last year?** (Understands their evolution)

### Understanding Scale & Challenges
- **What are the biggest operational challenges your team faces right now?** (Understands real problems, not just idealized version)
- **How do you handle scaling? Are there specific bottlenecks you're watching?**
- **What's the most complex feature or system you currently maintain?**

---

## About the Team & How They Work

### Team Structure & Dynamics
- **How is the team structured? How many backend vs. frontend developers?**
- **How do you collaborate between backend and frontend on features?** (Shows interest in cross-team coordination)
- **Is there someone who would be my primary mentor/buddy during onboarding?**
- **What's the team size, and how is it expected to grow?**

### Processes & Workflow
- **Walk me through how a feature goes from idea to production. What does a typical sprint look like?**
- **How do you handle code reviews? What's the feedback culture like?**
- **What's your approach to testing? Unit tests, integration tests, end-to-end?**
- **How do you manage on-call responsibilities and incident response?** (Especially relevant since you have on-call experience)
- **Are there pair-programming sessions, and if so, how often?**

### Learning & Knowledge Sharing
- **How do you typically onboard new team members? Is there a structured plan or more organic?**
- **What resources or tools do you use to document architecture decisions and system design?**
- **Are there regular team learning sessions (tech talks, code reviews as learning)?**
- **If I'm stuck on something, what's the culture around asking for help?** (Shows you're practical about asking questions)
- **Has anyone else on the team learned Python/Vue on the job? How was that experience?**

---

## About Technology & Architecture

### Python Backend Context
- **What version of Python are you running, and are you planning upgrades soon?**
- **What frameworks and libraries are you using in Python? (FastAPI, Django, etc.?)**
- **How is your Python codebase structured? Any architectural patterns I should know about?**
- **What does testing look like in your Python services? Any specific tools or practices?**
- **How do you handle Python dependency management and virtual environments?**

### Vue Frontend Context
- **What version of Vue are you using (Vue 2 or 3)?**
- **Are there any specific libraries or design systems I should familiarize myself with?**
- **How do you structure your Vue components? Any architectural guidelines?**
- **How do your backend and frontend teams coordinate on API contracts?**

### Infrastructure & Deployment
- **Tell me about your DynamoDB schemas and access patterns. What's the biggest lesson you've learned?** (Shows you understand serverless data)
- **What's your CI/CD pipeline look like? How often do you deploy?** (Relevant since you've worked with CI/CD)
- **How is your infrastructure managed? (IaC, CDK, Terraform, CloudFormation?)**
- **What's your approach to secrets management and environment configuration?**
- **How do you monitor and alert on issues?** (Relevant for on-call and reliability)

### Technical Debt & Modernization
- **Are there any parts of the codebase you're planning to refactor or modernize?**
- **What's the biggest technical constraint you're working around right now?**

## About Day-to-Day Work & Growth

### Actual Day-to-Day
- **What would my first features look like as I'm ramping up?** (Understand the ramp-up path)
- **How much time do you spend on meetings vs. coding?**
- **What's the typical pull request workflow?** (How long does review take, what's expected?)
- **Are there support/maintenance tasks everyone rotates through?**

### Growth & Expectations
- **What would be expected of me after 3 months? 6 months?**
- **What does career progression look like on this team?** (How do people grow?)
- **Are there opportunities to lead features or projects as I ramp up?**
- **What skills or areas would be most valuable for me to invest in learning?**

## About Culture & Team Fit

### Team Dynamics
- **What's something you enjoy most about working on this team?**
- **What's the biggest challenge about working here?** (Honest answer reveals culture)
- **How does the team handle disagreements or technical decisions?** (Decision-making process)
- **What's the team's approach to work-life balance?** (Remote-friendly? Flexible hours?)

### Integration with Larger Company
- **How much autonomy does your team have in technical decisions?**
- **What's the relationship like between your team and other Otto Group teams?**
- **Are there cross-team initiatives or dependencies I should be aware of?**

## Strategic Questions (Shows Thoughtfulness)

- **What's the vision for this team's tech stack over the next 1-2 years?** (Are you exploring new tech, or consolidating?)
- **If you could change one thing about how the team works, what would it be?**
- **What's surprised you most about working on this team since you joined?**
- **Why is this role opening up? What's the previous person doing now?** (Understands team trajectory)

## Tips for Asking

✅ **Ask naturally throughout conversation** - Don't fire off a list. Weave them in based on what they're telling you.

✅ **Follow up with "why?"** - If they mention something interesting, dig deeper. This shows genuine curiosity.

✅ **Listen more than you talk** - This is a get-to-know, so give them space to tell their story.

✅ **Ask about challenges, not just highlights** - Every team has them. Teams that acknowledge challenges honestly are healthier.

✅ **Show you've done your homework** - Reference something about Otto Group or the team you learned beforehand (without making it awkward).

✅ **Ask questions that show you're thinking about contribution** - "What would I be working on first?" shows you care about adding value.

---

## Questions That Reveal Red Flags (Listen for These Answers)

- **"We don't really have tests"** → Quality/speed concerns
- **"Everyone's burned out from on-call"** → Reliability issues, poor tooling
- **"We don't pair program or help each other much"** → Siloed team, harder onboarding
- **"We just migrate from [language] to Python and there's no one who knows Python well yet"** → You might be alone in learning
- **"Code review is slow/contentious"** → Culture or process issues
- **"We rarely deploy"** → Risk-averse, slow feedback loops

---

## Example Follow-Up Pattern

**You ask:** "How do you typically onboard new team members?"

**They say:** "We have them read some docs and pair with someone for the first week."

**You follow up:** "That's great. When someone gets stuck—like, really stuck on understanding how the architecture works—what does that conversation look like? Do you encourage questions early, or do people try to figure it out themselves first?"

This shows you're thinking practically about learning, not just asking surface-level questions.
